Catalogue images, made automatically

The catalogue renderer opens this viewer on a server, sets up every head and band that fit in each metal, waits for the finished image and saves it. These are its output, ready for product pages.

Fit check: every head on every shank

  • How it works: each of your files is a ring your designer joined on purpose, so the app learns from it where the head and shank meet. It then seats every other head on that shank and measures, in millimetres, how much of each arm's joint the new head covers and how much of the head rests on the band.
  • Result: a mix is offered only if both arms and the head are at least 85% covered. Between 60% and 85% it would go to a short review list; below that it is never shown.

What the app found in your files

    How it is drawn now

    • Metal is lit by studio softboxes and uses the exact surface curves saved in your CAD files, so it reflects smoothly.
    • Shading in crevices is worked out in advance: under the halo, between prongs and inside the split shank the metal darkens, as it does in a photo.
    • Stones use the real facets from your files. Light is followed as it bends into the stone, bounces inside and comes back out. Your cuts are all convex, so the exit facet is found exactly and cheaply.
    • Photo quality path traces the metal: light bounces between metal parts, so the band reflects the head and prongs, with real shadows. The stones keep the exact facet tracing, fire included.
    • Smaller download: the 3D data is compressed with the codec glTF files use, about a third of its former size.
    • Smooth and sharp: while moving, quality adapts to the device so turning stays fluid; when still, 24 slightly shifted frames are averaged for clean facets and edges, with full rainbow fire.
    • Each stone bends light by its real amount: diamond 2.42, sapphire and ruby 1.77, emerald 1.58. That is why diamonds sparkle most.
    • Colour deepens where light travels further inside a stone, as in real gems.
